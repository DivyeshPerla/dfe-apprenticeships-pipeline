# AI layer

Two capabilities, both chosen because the *data problem* justifies them rather
than because AI is fashionable.

---

## 1. Regional segmentation (BigQuery ML k-means)

`gold.region_segments` — clusters the nine English regions by the **shape** of
their apprenticeship market, using level shares rather than volumes so the
result reflects composition, not size.

| cluster | regions | intermediate | advanced | higher |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | London | 0.209 | 0.418 | **0.373** |
| 1 | East of England | 0.255 | 0.432 | 0.313 |
| 3 | the other seven | 0.26–0.36 | ~0.43 | 0.23–0.29 |

**Finding:** London separates on its own — the highest share of higher
apprenticeships (37.3%) and the lowest share of intermediate (20.9%).
Yorkshire and The Humber is the most intermediate-weighted (36.4%).

Deliberately excluded from training: the "Outside of England and unknown"
residual bucket (not a place), and part-year 2025/26 (would distort shares).

Runs as ordinary BigQuery compute, so it sits inside the free query tier.

---

## 2. Suppression-aware narratives (Gemini via BigQuery remote model)

`gold.regional_narratives` — a two-sentence summary per region, generated by
`ML.GENERATE_TEXT` against `gemini-2.5-flash` through a Vertex AI connection.

**Why this is the differentiator.** Most LLM-over-warehouse demos will happily
invent a plausible number for a withheld cell. This one cannot, because the
architecture prevents it: suppressed measures are `NULL` in silver and the
*reason* travels alongside in a status column, so the prompt renders `WITHHELD`
rather than a value. The model is then instructed to say a figure is not
published and explain that this is disclosure control.

Every one of the nine narratives correctly reports its suppression count.

### The first version was wrong, and that is the interesting part

The initial run produced narratives saying starts were **"projected to
increase"** — for academic years that have already happened. It also wrote
"the Midlands" for East Midlands. The suppression guardrail held perfectly;
the *framing* was invented.

Fixed by hardening the prompt: an explicit statement that these are published
historical figures, a ban on forecast vocabulary, and a requirement to use the
exact region name. Second run: **0 of 9** narratives used forecast language.

**Verification, not trust.** Every figure in the output was checked against
`mart_regional_trends` — London 36,870 → 40,400 with 20,810 achievements,
North West 58,140 → 50,590, Yorkshire 44,590 → 38,490 — all exact. An LLM
writing prose over your warehouse still needs its numbers audited, and the
guardrail that matters is the one in the data model, not the one in the prompt.

### Cost

The only non-free component in the project. Bounded to 9 rows x 1 call, run on
demand rather than on a schedule.
